Catch the winter damage.

Spring maintenance is a walk-around with a purpose. Start at the roof line, follow the water down the gutters and away from the foundation, then check the systems that sat idle all winter.

The spring shortlist

  • Clear gutters and confirm water runs away from the house.
  • Look for missing shingles and lifted flashing.
  • Service the AC and clear the condensate line.
  • Repair screens, caulk, paint, and deck boards.
  • Check grading, downspouts, and any sign of basement moisture.

Do not pressure-wash delicate siding or walk a steep roof to save a service call. The risk is not worth it.

Good questions.

Short answers. Local codes and licensed trades still decide the details.

What is on a spring home maintenance checklist?

Gutters, roof and flashing, AC service, window screens, exterior paint touch-ups, deck cleaning, grading and drainage, and irrigation start-up. The exact list depends on your climate and features.

When should I turn on the AC?

Test it before the first hot week. A spring tune-up can catch a weak capacitor, a dirty condenser, or a clogged condensate line while the schedule is still flexible.

What should I look for after winter?

Water stains, missing shingles, lifted flashing, cracked caulk, frost damage, and soil that has settled against the foundation. Take photos before repairs so you can track changes.

How do I check grading?

The ground should slope away from the foundation for at least the first few feet. Look for low spots, mulch piled against siding, and downspouts that discharge next to the house.
