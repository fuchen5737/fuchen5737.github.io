Before the first freeze.

Fall work is mostly about water and heat. Water left in hoses, irrigation lines, and exterior pipes can freeze and split. Heat that has not been serviced can fail on the coldest night. Gutters full of leaves can send water into the fascia and foundation.

The fall shortlist

  • Clear gutters and check downspouts.
  • Look at the roof, flashing, and chimney from the ground.
  • Book the furnace tune-up and replace the filter.
  • Drain hoses, shut off outdoor water, and winterize irrigation.
  • Insulate vulnerable pipes and seal drafts.

If you heat with a fireplace or wood stove, get the flue inspected before the first fire. Creosote and blocked flues are not a wait-and-see item.

Good questions.

Short answers. Local codes and licensed trades still decide the details.

What is on a fall home maintenance checklist?

Gutters, roof flashing, furnace service, fireplace and chimney inspection, weatherstripping, pipe insulation, outdoor hose shutdown, irrigation winterization, and a final exterior walk-around.

When should I schedule a furnace tune-up?

Early fall, before the first cold snap. Technicians book up quickly once the weather turns; a pre-season visit is easier to schedule and can catch a cracked heat exchanger or dirty burners.

Do I need to winterize irrigation?

In freezing climates, yes. Water left in the lines can crack valves, backflow preventers, and heads. Use the manufacturer’s blow-out procedure or call a professional.

What should I do about ice dams?

Air-seal and insulate the attic, keep soffit vents open, and clear gutters. If ice dams recur, get a professional assessment; roof raking alone is a temporary fix and can be dangerous.
