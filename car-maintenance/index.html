      A checklist is a list. A schedule is a decision.

      Most car maintenance content tells you to “change the oil every 5,000 to 7,500 miles” and leaves you to do the math. The hard part is not knowing that oil exists. It is knowing what is due before a busy month, a road trip, or a warning light turns a routine job into an emergency.

      Milemark starts with your current mileage and annual pace. It turns each interval into a due date and a due odometer reading, then sorts the next year. You can see the next job, the ones that can wait, and the monthly habits that prevent surprises.

      Good questions.

      Short answers. The owner’s manual still wins.

      Is this a manufacturer maintenance schedule?

      No. It is a general planner based on common service intervals. Your owner’s manual and any factory service schedule are the final authority, especially for oil spec, fluids, and warranty requirements.

      How are the due dates calculated?

      We add the interval to your current odometer, convert the mileage interval into a date using your annual mileage, and also apply a time interval. The earlier of the two becomes the due date.

      What counts as severe driving?

      Towing, hauling, frequent short trips, stop-and-go traffic, dusty roads, mountains, and extreme heat or cold are common severe-duty conditions. The planner shortens the intervals for oil, filters, and some fluids.

      Can I use this for an EV or hybrid?

      Yes. Choose the powertrain and engine-only jobs such as oil and spark plugs disappear. EVs still need tires, brakes, cabin filters, coolant, and charging-equipment checks; hybrids add inverter cooling.

      Where is my log saved?

      In this browser’s local storage. It does not go to a server. Clearing browser data or switching devices will clear it, so print the sheet if you want a permanent copy.

      Does this replace a mechanic?

      No. It helps you ask better questions and avoid missed routine work. Sounds, smells, warning lights, and safety concerns should be inspected by a qualified mechanic now, not on a schedule.
