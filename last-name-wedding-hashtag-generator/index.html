    Three moves for one last name.

    Use the family name as a noun, a verb, or a team. The best one is the version you can say out loud without explaining it.

    01

    Pluralize it

    The Smiths. Meet the Smiths. The Smith Party of Two. Simple, readable, and easy to spell.

    02

    Pun it

    Happily Ever Smith. Smithly Ever After. Smith in Love. One clean turn of phrase, not three.

    03

    Make it a team

    Team Smith. Smith 2027. The Smith Chapter. Short tags work on signs, napkins, and photo-booth frames.

    Good questions.

    Short answers, no wedding-planning fluff.

    What if the last name is hard to spell?

    Keep the main tag simple and put the clever version on the reception signs. Guests should not have to ask how to spell the tag.

    Should we use one partner’s last name or both?

    Use the name you are both happy to repeat. If you are keeping two names, a first-name-plus-initials tag is often easier for guests.

    Can we use a last name that ends in S?

    Yes. The generator adds the right plural ending, so Smith becomes Smiths and Jones becomes Joneses.

    Can we use a last name for a same-sex wedding?

    Yes. The formulas are not gendered: plural, pun, team, and date tags work with any two names or any family name.
