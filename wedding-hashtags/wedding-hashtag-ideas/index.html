Wedding hashtag ideas you can make your own.

A working list of formulas, not a list of stolen tags. Mix your names, your last name, your date, and one inside joke. The generator turns any of these into a full proof sheet.

Start with the two names

The safest tags are the ones guests can reconstruct from the invitation. Put both names in, then decide whether you want “and,” “weds,” or nothing at all.

  • #AnnaAndBenboth names, clean
  • #AnnaWedsBena little ceremony
  • #AnnaBenEverAftertwo names plus a cliché
  • #ABSayIDoinitials, short

Put the last name to work

The last name is the most reusable ingredient: plural, pun, team, or headline. If the spelling is tricky, keep the main tag on the simpler version and use the clever one on the reception signs.

  • #TheSmithsplural, classic
  • #MeetTheSmithsplural plus a phrase
  • #HappilyEverSmithpun
  • #SmithPartyOfTwopun plus the guest count
  • #TeamSmithshort
  • #Smith2027last name plus the year

Borrow a cliché and twist it

A familiar phrase with one wrong turn is funnier than a completely invented phrase. Keep the turn obvious enough that guests get it on the first read.

  • #YouMayNowScrollthe ceremony line, updated
  • #LastFirstKissa small inversion
  • #NoMoreSwipingthe dating-app joke
  • #HappilyEverAfterPartythe reception, promised
The two-drink testThe best hashtag is the one your guests can spell after two drinks. If you have to explain the spelling, put it on a sign instead of asking people to type it.

Use the date

Date tags are easy to reuse for anniversaries and easy for guests to remember. Pick the month and year if the exact day feels too long.

  • #June2027month plus year
  • #June12Smithsday plus the family name
  • #SummerOfSmithseason plus the last name
  • #Smith2027short and anniversary-ready

Use the place or the story

This is where the tag stops sounding like a template. A dog park, a lake, a terrible first date, a favorite taco truck — one concrete detail beats three abstract adjectives.

  • #FromDogParkToSmiththe origin story
  • #LoveAtTheLakethe place
  • #OurDogParkStorythe detail, owned
  • #TacoTruckVowsthe reception, honestly

Before you commit

  • Search the exact tag on Instagram. If another couple is already using it, add the year or a middle name.
  • Check the spelling out loud, especially if the last name is easy to mistype.
  • Pick one official tag and use it everywhere: invitations, signage, photo booth, thank-you cards.
  • Keep a shorter backup for guests who are typing on a phone.
