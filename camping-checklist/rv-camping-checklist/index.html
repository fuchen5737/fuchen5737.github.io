Set up, then settle in.

An RV checklist has two halves: the campsite setup and the people inside. The setup half is where expensive mistakes happen: driving away with the awning out, the power cord connected, or the steps down.

Before you leave the site

  • Awning in, windows closed, roof vents down.
  • Water hose, sewer hose, and power cord disconnected and stowed.
  • Leveling blocks and chocks removed; steps stowed.
  • Bins, chairs, and the picnic table checked for leftovers.
  • Tanks capped and the site left cleaner than you found it.

Use a carbon monoxide detector, respect generator hours, and never dump tanks anywhere except an approved station.

Good questions.

Short answers. Check the campsite rules before you pack the axe.

What is different about an RV checklist?

An RV trip adds leveling, hookups, tanks, and a rolling kitchen. The list includes leveling blocks, chocks, a fresh-water hose, a sewer hose, a power cord, a surge protector, a fire extinguisher, and a carbon monoxide detector.

Do I need a sewer hose at every campsite?

Only if you have a full hookup or plan to dump tanks. Pack it with gloves and follow the campground’s rules. Never dump gray or black water on the ground.

What should I check before leaving the campsite?

Walk the site: awning in, hoses disconnected, power cord unplugged, steps stowed, levelers removed, and nothing left on the picnic table. A departure checklist prevents the most common RV damage.

Can I use this list for a motorhome and a trailer?

Yes. It covers the shared systems: leveling, water, power, tanks, and safety. Add any vehicle-specific tools or spare parts your manual calls for.
