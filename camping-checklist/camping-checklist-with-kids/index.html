Pack for the kids, not the photo.

Camping with children is mostly about margin: extra clothes, extra wipes, extra snacks, and a plan for the moment someone is cold, wet, or done with the day. A familiar comfort item earns its space.

Family-trip rules that help

  • One bag per child, with a full change of clothes in a dry bag.
  • Layers, not one heavy coat; kids run hot and cold.
  • Headlamps and a night light make the dark less dramatic.
  • Choose a site with a bathroom and a short walk if you have very young children.
  • Bring more snacks than you think you need; hangry is a real weather system.

Keep the first trip close to home and short. A good night’s sleep beats a perfect itinerary.

Good questions.

Short answers. Check the campsite rules before you pack the axe.

What should I pack for camping with kids?

Start with the normal tent or car list, then add extra clothes, wipes, snacks, games, a night light, and a familiar comfort item. The builder adds the kid-specific items and increases quantities for the group.

How do I keep kids warm at night?

Use a sleeping bag rated for the conditions, a warm base layer, a hat, and a dry change of clothes. Avoid overdressing before bed; damp clothes make the night colder.

What is the best camping age?

Families camp with babies and teens, but the plan changes. With very young children, choose a site close to the car and a bathroom, and bring more wipes and changes of clothes than you think you need.

Should I bring toys?

A few familiar, weather-tolerant toys are worth the space. Add a night light and a card game; leave the giant toy bin at home.
